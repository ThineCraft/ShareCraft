GridCraft co-authoring: the proofs

Before anything is built for live co-authoring in GridCraft, plan G1 tested the four things the adopted design rests on (C16, “proofs first”). This report gives each result, its evidence, and a recommendation: go, with conditions.

01 / Recommendation

Go, with conditions. The core of the design holds. Two copies of the engine given the same changes end in the same workbook, the browser build agrees with the desktop on everything people type, and saving is deterministic. Three results change details of the design rather than its shape:

  • Computed values can differ in the last digit between desktop and browser (64 of 1,584 maths cells, all by the smallest possible step; 3 of them visible as 15-digit text). Agreement checks must not compare computed values across platforms, or both builds must use the same maths library. Desktop editors stay out of shared sessions until one of these is in place, as C16 already says.
  • Re-running a row or column insert or delete on a 100,000-cell workbook takes about the whole desktop budget (43 to 60 ms against 50 ms). Cell edits and formatting re-run in under 1 ms each. G3 either makes structural re-runs cheaper or uses the design’s “wait for the server” mode for structural changes on large workbooks.
  • Save and reopen is lossless only if the reopened file is recalculated, and 8 of 10,000 random histories still lose a row height. Checkpoints must be loaded with a full recalculation, and the row-height loss is fixed in G4.

None of these needs a different architecture. They are listed with the plan that owns each in section 10; the owner’s decisions are in section 12.

03 / Two engines agree

Each random history is a list of shared commands with explicit sheets and ranges, never relying on the selection, drawn by a seeded generator: 1 to 6 sheets, formulas from 40 common functions including cross-sheet references, and about 15 % structural changes. Two engines in one process apply it. After every step their outcome and two SHA-256 fingerprints are compared: entered (what people typed and formatted, sheet structure, names) and computed (every calculated value, by its exact bits). Before every step the second engine’s selection and active sheet are moved at random, so a command that secretly reads the selection would show up.

Scope: identical histories from a new workbook, in one process, on one platform. Filters are not driven (section 07). It proves nothing yet about rolling back and re-applying against applying straight through, or about a client that loaded a checkpoint; those are G3 and G4 tests.

04 / Desktop and browser

The browser build (WebAssembly) replays the same 26 stored histories and compares them against fingerprints recorded on the desktop. All agree, entered and computed. Because those histories use few maths functions, a separate grid was added: 36 maths formulas over 44 inputs, covering logarithms, exponentials, square roots, fractional powers, trigonometric and hyperbolic functions and their inverses, and number display near powers of ten.

CheckResult
Entered contentIdentical everywhere
Computed values64 of 1,584 cells differ, every one by exactly 1 ULP, about one part in 1016. POWER 21, ^ 6, ATAN2 8, ATANH 7, COSH 6, SINH 4, ACOSH 3, ASINH 3, TANH 2; ASIN, SIN, COS and TAN 1 each. LN, LOG, LOG10, EXP, SQRT, ACOS, ATAN and ROUND agree exactly.
Displayed textThe same under General, #,##0.00 and 0.0000000000. At 15 significant digits (joining with text, the formula bar, a 15-digit scientific format), 3 cells differ: SIN(π/4) shows 0.707106781186548 on the desktop and 0.707106781186547 in the browser, POWER(2,-0.5) the same, and 1e5^0.37 5.49540873857625 against 5.49540873857624.
How it is guardedEvery value difference and every text difference is in a committed list that is checked exactly, so a new or vanished difference fails the test. A failure names the root cell, every function in its formula with its evaluated arguments, and the precedents’ values on both sides.

What it means. The desktop uses the platform’s maths library and the browser uses Rust’s. A 1-ULP difference is invisible in a normal cell, but it shows in 15-digit text, and at a rounding boundary it could change a comparison, a ROUND or a displayed value (the grid did not hit one). So the computed fingerprint cannot be the agreement check across platforms. Section 12 of the approach already named the answer: one maths library written in Rust in both builds. Until then, desktop editors stay out of shared sessions (C16).

Measured on x86_64 Windows (MSVC) against wasm32 under Node 24, with the test runner 0.2.129. Linux and macOS desktops and real browsers were not measured.

05 / Save and reopen

Every history’s final workbook was saved as .xlsx, reopened and compared three ways: saving twice gives identical bytes; reopening and recalculating gives the same fingerprints; and save, reopen, save gives the same bytes again. The requested edge cases were included: sheet names that need quoting, renamed and deleted sheets referred to by formulas, very large and very small numbers, empty strings against empty cells, and merged areas.

CheckResult
Saving is deterministicPassed on all 10,034 cases
Lossless after reopening with recalculationPassed except 8 of 10,000 sequences (0.08 %): reopening re-fits a row height from the values (wrapped formula text grew, or #CIRC! stored as 0). Kept as a test that fails once the loss is fixed.
Reopened from stored values onlyNot lossless: #CIRC! comes back as 0, and #SPILL! and #CALC! as #VALUE!. A checkpoint must therefore be loaded with a full recalculation.
Fixed in G1Empty column records are no longer written; row heights are written precisely enough to read back, and above 409.5 pt in hundredths; borders re-fit their rows; the fingerprint skips blank default cells and reads dynamic arrays the way a file writes them.

Filters are not covered, because the filter commands are not yet shared (section 07).

06 / Rolling back and re-running

When someone else’s change arrives before yours is agreed, the editor goes back to the agreed workbook and re-applies its own pending changes on top. The timed work is: copy the agreed workbook, then apply the pending commands, each with the recalculation it normally does. Release builds, one discarded warm-up, 30 measured runs, p95. The desktop ran three times as separate processes, at high priority on the performance cores. The workbook has two 100-column sheets, 20 % formulas, whole-column sums and cross-sheet references.

100,000 cells, 10 pending changesDesktop p95Browser build p95 (Node)Budget

One command alone: row or column insert or delete43 to 60 ms (p50)33 to 39 msAbout the whole desktop budget

What it means. Re-running ordinary edits is cheap. A structural change is expensive because it rewrites references and recalculates the whole workbook. G3 chose the design’s “wait for the server” mode for structural changes on large workbooks and proved rollback/re-run equal to straight-through application.

Qualifiers: the workbook’s formulas are cheap, so real workbooks may be slower. The browser figure is one run under Node, not pinned and not in a real browser. The browser build being faster than the desktop is unexplained; the copy alone takes 1.0 to 1.3 ms on the desktop against 0.3 ms, which points at the Windows memory allocator, not yet tested. A first figure of 97 ms included an extra full recalculation the design never does; it is kept in the evidence and not used here.

G3 heavier-formula follow-up

G3 chose the wait-for-server path for structural commands, including structural Undo and Redo, on workbooks with at least 100,000 stored cells. Its equality test proves rollback and re-run reach the same result as straight-through application, including Undo/Redo.

07 / Commands that read the selection

A shared command must act only on what it is told, never on the sender’s or receiver’s selection. G1 found the cases below; G3 resolved them for the first shared command set:

CommandWhat it readsConsequence
Filter on and off (data.filter)A toggle that looks for a table at the active cellG3 keeps the toggle local and added explicit, idempotent set/remove commands.
data.filterBy, data.clearFilter, data.reapplyNo sheet parameter: always the active sheetG3 added explicit sheet and filter-range targets.
Underline and double underlineThe active cell’s underline, even when told on or offG3 sends the explicit resulting state and style.
Increase and decrease font sizeSteps from the active cell’s sizeG3 sends the resulting size.
Format CellsChecks the change against the active cell’s styleG3 validates the patch against each explicit target.
Bold, italic, wrap text, align left, centre and rightToggle from the active cell when “on” is not givenShared only in the explicit form, as proved in G1.
Insert and delete sheetMake the new sheet active; reset the selectionG3’s apply loop preserves each co-author’s view.

08 / Determinism audit

Every hash map, clock, random source, use of a memory address and floating-point path in the engine, model, calculation, formula, functions and xlsx code was classed as safe, fixed in G1, or left for G3, with the reason. G3 fixed the clock, random state, year-less-date and shared-history items for its first command set; the maths-library and non-shared command findings remain open. The full current audit is docs/coauthor-determinism.md in the GridCraft fork.

G1 findingWhy it mattersStatus after G3
NOW and TODAYEach client has its own clockFixed for G3: the server stamp sets the agreed calculation clock.
RAND, RANDBETWEEN, RANDARRAYThe random state lives in the calculator, not the workbook, so a rollback or reload draws other numbersFixed for G3: snapshots carry the RNG cursor and the server stamp seeds the agreed stream.
Dates typed without a yearRead the system clock, so two clients either side of New Year differFixed for G3: cell.set carries the origin year; formula and filter coercion use the stamped clock.
Maths library differencesSection 04Open: agreement uses entered content only until both builds use one maths library.
Conditional formats, validation lists, Advanced Filter and pivot calculated fieldsUse the system clock and a fixed random seedPass the session’s clock and seed
Iterative calculation, manual calculation, more than 8 spill roundsResults depend on calculation historyA full recalculation after each agreed step, or per-workbook settings
AutoFit column width, password salts, the random-number tool, macro time limits, cell picturesDepend on the machine, and are not shared commandsThe originator sends the result
Undo history depthDiffers between platformsFixed for G3: personal history is outside snapshots and agreement and uses content-based count and byte limits.

11 / Not proved

  • Real browsers: every browser-build result ran under Node.
  • Linux and macOS desktops.
  • Clients that loaded a checkpoint, beyond the G4 fork tests in section 10: equal hashes from two fresh editors, and checkpoint-plus-suffix against straight-through, are not proved yet.
  • Expensive-formula workbooks outside the G3 follow-up’s deliberately heavy 20,000-formula, one-80-cell-block workload.
  • A 1-ULP difference at a rounding boundary changing a comparison or a displayed value.
  • Why the browser build is faster than the desktop.

Open items after G3

  • I7 · G5: identity tables grow and become expensive under fragmentation; index and share them, and cap distinct structural blocks.
  • I8 · G4: closed in G4. Deletion ownership is saved with checkpoint identities (section 10).
  • Maths library: the last-digit desktop/browser differences in section 04 remain open.
  • Pre-existing GridCraft gates: clippy at apps/gridcraft-cli/src/check.rs:281, complex_numbers, caret_after_typing_arabic_is_drawn_at_its_left_end, and the stale keytips table all fail at the fork’s G3 base as well as at its tip.

Changes in version 0.2

Version 0.2 adds the reviewed G3 results from the GridCraft fork (19 November 2026). It marks the G3-owned conditions met with their tests, adds the heavier-formula workload and timings, and carries the checkpoint, fragmented-identity, maths-library and pre-existing gate findings forward. It does not change the G1 measurements or the original go-with-conditions recommendation.

Changes in version 0.3

Version 0.3 adds the G4 checkpoint results from the GridCraft fork (19 November 2026). Checkpoints now load with a full recalculation, the G1 row-height loss is fixed and its test runs, and deletion ownership travels with the checkpoint identities. The fork gate run for G4 Task 12 (cargo test -p gridcraft-engine --test it coauthor_ on coauthor-g5 at 70dac9e9, which contains G4) passed 173 tests with 3 ignored release-only tests. Still open: the second-writer and checkpoint-plus-suffix proofs, entered-content agreement after a checkpoint between the native and browser builds, one maths library, real browsers and the G5 host bridge. The G1 measurements and the recommendation are unchanged.
