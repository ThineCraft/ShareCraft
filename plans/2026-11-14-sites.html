Sites: templates, lifecycle, hubs, navigation, language and time zone: implementation plan

ShareCraft · Stage 2, platform plan 3B · Written 14 November 2026

For agentic workers: REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: use superpowers:subagent-driven-development or superpowers:executing-plans. Steps are checkboxes.

Goal: sites that work like SharePoint's. An administrator creates a site from a built-in Team or Communication template or from a template saved from another site, and can carry templates between installations as a JSON package. Each site has a description, a language and a time zone, and shows its owners. Sites can be archived (read-only) and deleted (restorable for 93 days), and libraries can be deleted to the recycle bin and restored. An administrator makes a site a hub; other sites join it and share its navigation bar. Every page shows the organisation's global navigation, the hub's bar and the site's own navigation, whose links can be aimed at groups. The home page becomes a site directory.

Architecture: each site gets a second Git record, sites/<xx>/<id>.json, beside its tree record: kind, description, language, time zone, archive stamp, hub navigation and joined hub, local navigation and the template it came from. Saved templates are templates/<xx>/<id>.json and the global navigation is org/navigation.json. All three are mirrored into PostgreSQL as the tree is (plan 1: one commit per change, mirrored after the commit, taken back if refused, compared at every start). Deleting a site is a soft delete of its site node, so the existing rule "a deleted node at or above means no access" already hides everything in it. Archiving narrows every decision of the one access function to view only. Navigation is answered by the server already filtered for the viewer, so a page never receives a name the viewer could not see.

Tech stack: the existing workspace; jiff 0.2 (MIT or Unlicense, already in Cargo.lock through another crate, with its bundled time-zone database on Windows) added to records to check time-zone names. No other new dependency.

Spec: scope.html SC-01 (team and communication sites, hub-style groupings, site templates and reusable provisioning packages, language settings, time zones, global and local navigation, breadcrumbs, site discovery, site ownership, lifecycle, archival, restoration and deletion; audience targeting personalises navigation while permissions independently control access), SC-02 (library lifecycle), D24, D29, D31. Plans 1, 2A, 2B and 3A are the base. Owner decisions of 14 November 2026: built-in Team and Communication templates plus templates saved from a site (libraries, folders, groups, settings and navigation, no files), exportable as a JSON package; deleting a site keeps it 93 days in a deleted-sites list from which administrators restore it, and archiving makes a site read-only and hidden from discovery until an administrator reactivates it; hubs as in SharePoint (an administrator makes a hub, a site joins at most one, one level, joining never changes access); each site stores a language and time zone used for dates and numbers, and translation of ShareCraft's own words comes later.

Proposals in this plan for the owner to check

These are choices the plan makes that the owner's answers did not settle. Approving the plan approves them; any can be changed before building.

  1. Only administrators create, archive, reactivate, delete, restore and purge sites, make hubs, and save, import and delete templates. Site creation is already administrator-only. SharePoint also lets owners of some sites delete them.
  2. A site's owners (people with manage_site) edit its description, language, time zone and navigation, join it to a hub they can see, and leave a hub.
  3. Team and Communication differ little for now. Both start with a Documents library and the three groups. A Communication site is shown with its own label. Their home pages will differ when pages arrive (Stage 3).
  4. An archived site is read-only for everyone, administrators included, until it is reactivated. Its anyone links stop working, and access requests on it are refused. Open editing sessions end with archived. SharePoint's archive makes a site unreachable; this plan keeps it readable by the people who could read it before.
  5. A deleted site is seen only by administrators, on an administrators' Sites page. When it is restored, everything comes back as it was: its bin with each entry's stage and days, its links, groups, members and quota. A site name taken meanwhile gets (2).

Not in this plan

  • Translating ShareCraft's interface into other languages; a person's own time zone and regional settings overriding the site's (later, once translation exists).
  • Site pages, home-page layouts, news and web parts (Stage 3, portals); site branding, themes and the hub's inherited look (plan 4). Previews, image and template libraries, upload rules, any file type, and malware scanning (plan 3C).
  • Hub permission sync, nested hubs, hubs limiting who may join, "following" sites, recently visited sites, and search across site content (search is its own stage).
  • Templates carrying files, permissions broken below the site, links or members (a template carries the shape only; the three site groups are always made empty, the creator joins Owners as today).

Global constraints

  • Site record in Git: sites/<first two hex digits>/<site id>.json, two-space indent, one line per field, in this field order: {"schema": 1, "id", "kind": "team"|"communication", "description": string (0–500 characters), "language": BCP 47 tag, "time_zone": IANA name, "template": id or built-in name or null, "archived": Stamp or null, "hub": {"navigation": [NavItem]} or null, "joined_hub": site id or null, "navigation": [NavItem] or null}. navigation: null means automatic: every library of the site, by name. A site created before this plan has no file; it is read as team, empty description, the organisation's default language and time zone, automatic navigation, and its file is written at the first change. refs/sharecraft/sites names the last commit that changed sites/, templates/ or org/.
  • Template record: templates/<xx>/<id>.json: {"schema": 1, "id", "name" (1–80), "description" (0–500), "created": Stamp, "deleted": Stamp or null, "body": TemplateBody}. TemplateBody = {"kind", "language", "time_zone", "libraries": [{"name", "folders": [{"name", "folders": […]}]}], "navigation": [NavItem] or null}. Built-in templates team and communication live in code, not Git, and cannot be deleted. A package is {"sharecraft_template": 1, "name", "description", "body"}, at most 1 MiB, at most 100 libraries, 5,000 folders in all and 20 levels deep, with names cleaned by the 3A rules. Unknown fields are refused. Node targets in a package's navigation are library names ({"library": "Documents"}), never identifiers, and are resolved to the new site's libraries when a site is created.
  • NavItem: {"id": uuid, "label": 1–60 characters, "target": {"node": id} | {"url": string} | null, "audience": [group id] (0–10), "children": [NavItem]}. A null target is a heading. Children go one level only. There are at most 100 items in one list. A URL is at most 2,000 characters and is https:, http:, mailto: or a path starting with a single /; anything else (javascript:, data:, //host) is refused with invalid_navigation. A local navigation's node targets must be in the same site. Hub and global navigation may target any site.
  • Navigation as answered: the server resolves each list for the viewer. It leaves out an item whose node the viewer cannot see or which is deleted. It leaves out an item with an audience the viewer is in none of, unless the viewer manages that navigation, in which case the item comes with "targeted": true. It leaves out a heading left with no children. Labels of node targets are the node's current name unless a label was given. Audience targeting never grants or refuses access; it only hides a link.
  • Regional settings: a language is a well-formed BCP 47 tag of at most 35 characters (^[a-zA-Z]{2,3}(-[a-zA-Z]{4})?(-([a-zA-Z]{2}|[0-9]{3}))?$), and the page offers a fixed list of about 40 common ones. A time zone is a name jiff knows. The organisation defaults are new settings default_language ("en") and default_time_zone ("UTC"), set by administrators through the existing settings API. Pages set <html lang> to the site's language and show dates, times and sizes with Intl in the site's language and time zone. A page outside a site uses the organisation defaults. The server keeps sending UTC times.
  • Archived: the one access decision (chain_with in records/src/scopes.rs) narrows every permission on a node of an archived site to VIEW, for everyone. Every SQL fragment that computes permissions for display (sites_for, the explorer, the bin listing) does the same. Every write answers 403 archived. Archiving ends every editing session in the site (RemovedReason::Archived) and abandons its upload sessions, releasing their reservations. Anyone links on an archived site answer as an expired link does. The sweeper still purges its bin on time.
  • Deleted site: its site node is soft-deleted (deleted_at, deleted_with = id, no bin_stage). Everything in it is unreachable by the existing dead-ancestor rule. Its groups are left out of every group list, its links and requests answer as if missing, and its sessions end with deleted. It is purged 93 days after its deletion, or when an administrator purges it. A purge tombstones every record of the site in Git in one commit. It then deletes its groups (with memberships), assignments, links, requests and its usage row, and leaves any hub it was in. If it was a hub, its members leave it. Only then does it erase each blob nothing else names. A purge is marked in sites.purging before it starts and is finished by the sweeper after a restart.
  • Library lifecycle: deleting a library takes it, with everything live in it, to the first stage of its site's bin as one entry. It needs manage_site on the site and delete on every place below with its own permissions (ForbiddenBelow otherwise), and restores as a folder does. Its name is freed meanwhile; a restored library whose name was taken gets (2).
  • Locking order (extending 3A): node rows top-down, then sites rows by id, then link rows, then site_usage, then groups by id, then roles by id. Joining a hub locks both site rows, lower id first.
  • Events: site.changed (fields changed, old and new values), site.archived, site.reactivated, site.deleted, site.restored, site.purged, hub.registered, hub.unregistered, hub.joined, hub.left, navigation.changed ({"which": "global"|"hub"|"local"}), template.saved, template.imported, template.deleted. Names in events are recorded as text, as before.
  • Errors (new codes, plain-English messages in common.js): archived 403, invalid_navigation 422, invalid_template 422 (with {"reason"}), invalid_language 422, invalid_time_zone 422, not_a_hub 409, is_hub 409 (a hub cannot join a hub), site_deleted 409 (an operation on a deleted site other than restore and purge), not_deleted 409 (restoring or purging a site that is not deleted).

Review focus

  1. Every write to an archived site is refused, however it arrives. That covers upload finish, a save through an already-open file session, text-room edits, sharing, links, approving a request, bin restore, bulk operations, copy into the site and moves out of it. Reads, downloads and the explorer keep working. (Task 3, archived_site_refuses_every_write, which walks a list of every write route; archiving_ends_open_sessions.)
  2. Navigation and hub lists never reveal what the viewer cannot see. This covers a link to a library they cannot open, a member site of a hub they have no access to, and a targeted item for someone outside its audience. Audience targeting never grants access: a person outside the audience who has access still opens the target by its address. (Task 5, navigation_never_names_what_viewer_cannot_see, hub_lists_only_visible_member_sites, audience_hides_but_never_grants.)
  3. Restoring a deleted site gives back everything as it was: bin entries with their stages and days, links working again, groups and members, quota and usage, and the hub it was in if the hub still exists. A site name taken meanwhile gets (2). (Task 4, restore_site_brings_back_everything.)
  4. Purging a deleted site erases only bytes nothing else names. A file copied to another site shares its blob, and the blob stays. A purge interrupted by a stop finishes at the next start. (Task 4, site_purge_keeps_blobs_other_sites_name, site_purge_resumes_after_restart.)
  5. A template package from elsewhere cannot do harm. Packages that are too deep, too many folders, bad names, javascript: links, unknown fields, foreign identifiers, or over 1 MiB are refused or cleaned with a reason. Creating a site from a template whose library a navigation link names but which was renamed in the package still makes the site and drops that link. (Task 2, hostile_template_package_refused, template_navigation_resolved_by_name.)
