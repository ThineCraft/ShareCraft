What this concept decides

  • The editor takes the whole window. Site navigation is left behind; one slim bar keeps the way back, the document name and the site's accent and mark.
  • ShareCraft owns the bar, the banners and the side panel. The editor owns everything in the canvas, including other people's cursors, which it draws in colours ShareCraft assigns.
  • Saving is automatic and always visible. One status, in the same place, in the same words in every editor. There is no save button.
  • A person is never identified by colour alone. Initials sit in every marker, and names on every cursor.
  • Removed access hides the document at once. The message says what was kept and offers the way back.
  • Problems appear as one banner under the bar, with at most two actions, and never as a pop-up over the work.

Still to settle

  • Whether "Version" is the right word for a saved point, and when a new one is made during a long session.
  • What "Changed elsewhere" should offer by default. This depends on the hybrid and offline decision in the scope (SC-16).
  • Whether working while disconnected is allowed at all, or the editor becomes read-only until the connection returns.
  • Comments and mentions: in the side panel here, or left to each editor.
  • How many people are shown before the row collapses to a count.
  • Whether the editors' own menus need hiding or changing so two "save" or "open" paths do not exist.
